1. The Human Body as a Signal Medium

The human body is electrically, mechanically, thermally, acoustically, and electromagnetically active.

Neurons produce electrical activity. Muscles generate measurable electrical potentials. Heart contractions move tissue. Breathing displaces the chest. Blood flow creates periodic mechanical signatures. Speech preparation activates muscles even when no audible voice is produced. The head and other tissue absorb electromagnetic energy and respond mechanically and thermally.

This means that the body can operate simultaneously as:

A biological system
A signal source
A reflecting surface
A modulation source
A transduction medium
A receiver
A measurable behavioral output

Modern sensing systems increasingly exploit these properties without requiring a traditional implanted device.

The sovereignty boundary is therefore larger than the skin.

It includes the electromagnetic, acoustic, optical, thermal, and computational environment surrounding the person.

2. The Microwave Auditory Effect

2.1 Internally perceived sound from external RF energy

The microwave auditory effect, also called the radio-frequency hearing effect or Frey effect, is the perception of sound induced by pulsed or modulated radio-frequency energy.

The perceived event may take the form of:

Clicking Buzzing Hissing Knocking Chirping Pressure-like auditory sensations More complex encoded sound

The physical pathway is generally described through rapid RF-energy absorption, minute thermoelastic expansion, and the generation of a pressure wave that reaches the inner-ear auditory system.

The important architectural fact is direct: An external electromagnetic signal can produce an auditory perception inside the head without an ordinary loudspeaker at the ear.

The original human work by Allan Frey and subsequent radio-frequency auditory research established the phenomenon as a bioelectromagnetic effect associated with pulsed RF exposure.

2.2 The head as an RF-to-acoustic transducer

United States Air Force Patent US6470214B1, titled Method and Device for Implementing the Radio Frequency Hearing Effect, explicitly treats the head as an RF-to-acoustic demodulating medium.

The patent describes:

Speech preprocessing
Suppressed-carrier modulation
Single-sideband and carrier-suppressed AM
RF absorption in the head
Thermal expansion and contraction
Acoustic-wave conduction to the inner ear
Recovery of intelligible subjective sound
Digital signal processing implementations

The patent states that its purpose is to encode speech so that the transmitted signal is intelligibly recovered through the radio-frequency hearing effect. The original assignee is listed as the United States Department of the Air Force.

A related patent, US6587729B2, is titled Apparatus for Audibly Communicating Speech Using the Radio Frequency Hearing Effect.

These patents are important because they move the subject beyond a generalized claim that pulsed RF produces clicks. They describe an engineering pipeline:

Speech → Preprocessing → RF modulation → Biological demodulation → Perceived speech

That architecture supplies a documented technical foundation for voice-delivery systems operating without an ordinary acoustic receiver.

2.3 Signal design is the intelligence layer

Raw energy is only one part of the system. A functional communication system requires:

Waveform generation
Speech encoding
Carrier selection
Pulse timing
Amplitude shaping
Distortion compensation
Directionality
Target localization

The Air Force patent specifically addresses speech distortion by preprocessing the source signal and compensating for the transfer characteristics of the RF-to-acoustic process.

This is the same general principle used throughout modern communications: A difficult physical channel becomes useful when the transmitted information is shaped around the characteristics of the channel.

Artificial intelligence expands that capability by learning target-specific and environment-specific compensation patterns rather than relying only on fixed signal-processing rules.

4. Silent Speech and Subvocal Signals

The human speech system begins operating before audible sound leaves the mouth.

Speech preparation and silent articulation can create measurable electrical activity in:

Jaw muscles
Facial muscles
Throat muscles
Neck muscles
Muscles around the ear
Articulatory structures

Surface electromyography captures these signals. Machine-learning systems can then classify the intended word or command.

4.1 Silent speech through headphones

A 2025 wireless silent-speech interface integrated four textile EMG electrodes into headphone earmuffs.

The electrodes captured speech-related neuromuscular activity, and an ESP32-S3-based wireless module processed and transmitted the signals. A neural-network architecture dynamically weighted the sensor channels to handle changing skin-electrode coupling and movement.

96% accuracy

across ten voice-free control words

This is an important miniaturization milestone. The sensing system moved from visible laboratory patches to an ordinary consumer form factor:

Headphones became a speech-intent sensor.

4.2 Silent speech through textile neckbands

The 2026 SilentWear project created a textile neck interface with fourteen differential EMG channels and on-device speech recognition.

Reported performance included:

Average cross-validated accuracy for silent speech

2.47 ms

Inference latency

20.5 mW

Total system power

27+ hours

Continuous operation

A separate fully dry EMG neckband reported silent-command classification using fourteen channels, low-power wireless acquisition, and between-session testing after repositioning.

Laboratory electrodes → Wearable textiles → Consumer form factors → On-device AI → Continuous operation

4.3 The significance of internal speech leakage

Silent-speech systems demonstrate that language does not need to become audible before it becomes machine-readable.

The privacy boundary therefore extends backward into:

Speech preparation Motor intention Subvocal activity Neuromuscular leakage Pre-audible articulation

When these signals are linked to identity, location, emotional state, browsing history, voice history, and behavioral models, even restricted command recognition becomes part of a much larger inference system.

6. Ambient Wireless Sensing

6.1 Wi-Fi as environmental perception

Wireless signals change when they interact with bodies, walls, furniture, movement, breathing, and posture.

Channel State Information captures detailed properties of the propagation path between transmitters and receivers.

A Wi-Fi system can therefore serve two roles at once:

1. Communications infrastructure

2. Environmental sensing infrastructure

The Person-in-WiFi project used ordinary Wi-Fi antenna sets and deep learning to perform body segmentation and pose estimation.

The researchers collected more than 100,000 frames across sixteen indoor scenes. Wi-Fi reflections and transmission paths were used to estimate human body structure without a conventional visual camera as the primary sensor.

The 2025 PulseFi system used low-cost commodity Wi-Fi hardware and Channel State Information to estimate:

Heart rate Breathing rate Apnea events

Its machine-learning pipeline processed wireless telemetry collected with ESP32 and Raspberry Pi hardware.

The wireless field is therefore no longer only a carrier for messages sent by devices. The field itself becomes a measurement surface.

6.2 Millimeter-wave human sensing

These systems provide detailed environmental and physiological observability under conditions where ordinary cameras may be obstructed, poorly illuminated, or intentionally avoided.

12. The Cry Wolf Discrediting Layer

The dossier identifies a reported operational pattern called Cry Wolf.

The sequence is:

  1. 1Deliver an obvious, extreme, ridiculous, or difficult-to-explain event.
  2. 2Ensure the target recognizes it.
  3. 3Encourage the target to describe it to another person.
  4. 4Use the unusual description to damage the target's credibility.
  5. 5Continue quieter or more technically subtle activity.
  6. 6Route every later report through the credibility damage created by the first event.

This acts as a counter-evidence system. The attack is not only the original signal or intervention. The attack includes control of how the report will be interpreted.

The target is placed inside a credibility trap:

Silence preserves the operation.
Reporting exposes the target to dismissal.
Detailed reporting is characterized as obsession.
Brief reporting is characterized as unsupported.
Technical language is characterized as fantasy.
Emotional language is characterized as instability.
Extensive documentation is itself used against the person.

The Cry Wolf layer belongs in the technical architecture because a covert system must manage not only delivery and concealment, but also attribution and witness credibility.
