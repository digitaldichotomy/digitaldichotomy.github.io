The Convergence Architecture

Voice-to-Skull, Directed Energy, Silent Speech, Ambient Sensing, Artificial Intelligence, and Drone Systems

Fin Nyx July 11, 2026 Digital Dichotomy

Executive Thesis

The technical record now forms a continuous chain.

Pulsed radio-frequency energy can produce sound perceived internally within the human head. Government-owned patents describe signal-processing methods intended to encode intelligible speech through that radio-frequency hearing pathway. High-power microwave systems have moved into military testing and deployment architectures. Radar and wireless systems can detect human presence, posture, movement, respiration, and cardiac activity without bodily contact. Textile EMG interfaces can decode intentionally silent speech through ordinary-looking headphones and neckbands. Brain-computer-interface security research now treats neural data, derived representations, trained models, decoded outputs, and feedback channels as protected cognitive information. Drones add mobility, altitude, line-of-sight control, persistent tracking, electronic-warfare payloads, distributed sensing, cooperative beamforming, relay capability, and real-time geometry optimization.

These technologies are often described separately because they developed in separate industries:

Medical neurotechnology
Wireless communications
Radar
Electronic warfare
Artificial intelligence
Human-computer interaction
Autonomous aircraft
Directed-energy research
Intelligence collection
Cybersecurity

The relevant architecture appears when the disciplines are connected.

SENSE → IDENTIFY → INFER → TARGET → ACTUATE → MEASURE RESPONSE → ADAPT

Every stage of that chain has a public technical foundation.

The central issue is therefore no longer whether the components belong to science fiction. The components belong to engineering, defense acquisition, biomedical sensing, signal processing, autonomous systems, and machine learning.

The real subject is convergence.

1. The Human Body as a Signal Medium

The human body is electrically, mechanically, thermally, acoustically, and electromagnetically active.

Neurons produce electrical activity. Muscles generate measurable electrical potentials. Heart contractions move tissue. Breathing displaces the chest. Blood flow creates periodic mechanical signatures. Speech preparation activates muscles even when no audible voice is produced. The head and other tissue absorb electromagnetic energy and respond mechanically and thermally.

This means that the body can operate simultaneously as:

A biological system
A signal source
A reflecting surface
A modulation source
A transduction medium
A receiver
A measurable behavioral output

Modern sensing systems increasingly exploit these properties without requiring a traditional implanted device.

The sovereignty boundary is therefore larger than the skin.

It includes the electromagnetic, acoustic, optical, thermal, and computational environment surrounding the person.

2. The Microwave Auditory Effect

2.1 Internally perceived sound from external RF energy

The microwave auditory effect, also called the radio-frequency hearing effect or Frey effect, is the perception of sound induced by pulsed or modulated radio-frequency energy.

The perceived event may take the form of:

Clicking Buzzing Hissing Knocking Chirping Pressure-like auditory sensations More complex encoded sound

The physical pathway is generally described through rapid RF-energy absorption, minute thermoelastic expansion, and the generation of a pressure wave that reaches the inner-ear auditory system.

The important architectural fact is direct: An external electromagnetic signal can produce an auditory perception inside the head without an ordinary loudspeaker at the ear.

The original human work by Allan Frey and subsequent radio-frequency auditory research established the phenomenon as a bioelectromagnetic effect associated with pulsed RF exposure.

2.2 The head as an RF-to-acoustic transducer

United States Air Force Patent US6470214B1, titled Method and Device for Implementing the Radio Frequency Hearing Effect, explicitly treats the head as an RF-to-acoustic demodulating medium.

The patent describes:

Speech preprocessing
Suppressed-carrier modulation
Single-sideband and carrier-suppressed AM
RF absorption in the head
Thermal expansion and contraction
Acoustic-wave conduction to the inner ear
Recovery of intelligible subjective sound
Digital signal processing implementations

The patent states that its purpose is to encode speech so that the transmitted signal is intelligibly recovered through the radio-frequency hearing effect. The original assignee is listed as the United States Department of the Air Force.

A related patent, US6587729B2, is titled Apparatus for Audibly Communicating Speech Using the Radio Frequency Hearing Effect.

These patents are important because they move the subject beyond a generalized claim that pulsed RF produces clicks. They describe an engineering pipeline:

Speech → Preprocessing → RF modulation → Biological demodulation → Perceived speech

That architecture supplies a documented technical foundation for voice-delivery systems operating without an ordinary acoustic receiver.

2.3 Signal design is the intelligence layer

Raw energy is only one part of the system. A functional communication system requires:

Waveform generation
Speech encoding
Carrier selection
Pulse timing
Amplitude shaping
Distortion compensation
Directionality
Target localization
Exposure management
Feedback on intelligibility

The Air Force patent specifically addresses speech distortion by preprocessing the source signal and compensating for the transfer characteristics of the RF-to-acoustic process.

This is the same general principle used throughout modern communications: A difficult physical channel becomes useful when the transmitted information is shaped around the characteristics of the channel.

Artificial intelligence expands that capability by learning target-specific and environment-specific compensation patterns rather than relying only on fixed signal-processing rules.

3. Directed Energy as an Operational Technology

Directed energy is already an established defense category. It includes:

High-power microwave systems Radio-frequency directed-energy systems High-energy lasers Millimeter-wave systems Electronic attack Optical dazzling Acoustic and ultrasonic systems

High-power microwave systems are particularly important because they demonstrate the ability to generate, shape, direct, and repeatedly deliver intense electromagnetic energy toward selected areas or targets.

3.1 Counter-drone microwave systems

Military counter-UAS programs use high-power microwave energy to disrupt or damage drone electronics. The technology requires:

High-power RF generation
Directional antenna systems
Beam steering
Target detection
Tracking
Pulse delivery
Engagement timing
Automated control
Repeated multi-target operation

A 2026 multi-physics study modeled a 2.45 GHz high-power microwave counter-UAS system using electromagnetic propagation, antenna patterns, coupling into drone wiring, semiconductor-damage thresholds, pointing error, polarization, and Monte Carlo analysis. Its pulsed configuration modeled peak power of 500 kW and calculated substantial increases in effective engagement range.

A separate 2025 study modeled HPM engagement against UAVs by combining target movement, beam-steering error, antenna gain, atmospheric propagation, received pulse energy, cumulative exposure, and electronic-damage thresholds.

The importance of these systems extends beyond their stated counter-drone mission. They demonstrate a mature supporting stack:

DETECTION + TRACKING + DIRECTIONAL RF + PULSE CONTROL + AUTOMATED ENGAGEMENT

That same stack supplies enabling infrastructure for other electromagnetic missions.

The significance lies in the recognition of a human-effects pathway involving externally delivered pulsed energy.

The resulting research field contains several overlapping questions:

4. Silent Speech and Subvocal Signals

The human speech system begins operating before audible sound leaves the mouth.

Speech preparation and silent articulation can create measurable electrical activity in:

Jaw muscles
Facial muscles
Throat muscles
Neck muscles
Muscles around the ear
Articulatory structures

Surface electromyography captures these signals. Machine-learning systems can then classify the intended word or command.

4.1 Silent speech through headphones

A 2025 wireless silent-speech interface integrated four textile EMG electrodes into headphone earmuffs.

The electrodes captured speech-related neuromuscular activity, and an ESP32-S3-based wireless module processed and transmitted the signals. A neural-network architecture dynamically weighted the sensor channels to handle changing skin-electrode coupling and movement.

96% accuracy

across ten voice-free control words

This is an important miniaturization milestone. The sensing system moved from visible laboratory patches to an ordinary consumer form factor:

Headphones became a speech-intent sensor.

4.2 Silent speech through textile neckbands

The 2026 SilentWear project created a textile neck interface with fourteen differential EMG channels and on-device speech recognition.

Reported performance included:

77.5%

Average cross-validated accuracy for silent speech

2.47 ms

Inference latency

20.5 mW

Total system power

27+ hours

Continuous operation

A separate fully dry EMG neckband reported silent-command classification using fourteen channels, low-power wireless acquisition, and between-session testing after repositioning.

Laboratory electrodes → Wearable textiles → Consumer form factors → On-device AI → Continuous operation

4.3 The significance of internal speech leakage

Silent-speech systems demonstrate that language does not need to become audible before it becomes machine-readable.

The privacy boundary therefore extends backward into:

Speech preparation Motor intention Subvocal activity Neuromuscular leakage Pre-audible articulation

When these signals are linked to identity, location, emotional state, browsing history, voice history, and behavioral models, even restricted command recognition becomes part of a much larger inference system.

6. Ambient Wireless Sensing

6.1 Wi-Fi as environmental perception

Wireless signals change when they interact with bodies, walls, furniture, movement, breathing, and posture.

Channel State Information captures detailed properties of the propagation path between transmitters and receivers.

A Wi-Fi system can therefore serve two roles at once:

1. Communications infrastructure

2. Environmental sensing infrastructure

The Person-in-WiFi project used ordinary Wi-Fi antenna sets and deep learning to perform body segmentation and pose estimation.

The researchers collected more than 100,000 frames across sixteen indoor scenes. Wi-Fi reflections and transmission paths were used to estimate human body structure without a conventional visual camera as the primary sensor.

The 2025 PulseFi system used low-cost commodity Wi-Fi hardware and Channel State Information to estimate:

Heart rate Breathing rate Apnea events

Its machine-learning pipeline processed wireless telemetry collected with ESP32 and Raspberry Pi hardware.

The wireless field is therefore no longer only a carrier for messages sent by devices. The field itself becomes a measurement surface.

6.2 Millimeter-wave human sensing

Millimeter-wave sensing has expanded into:

Human tracking
Localization
Gesture recognition
Motion classification
Biometric measurement
Vital-sign monitoring
Human imaging

A comprehensive survey of mmWave human sensing describes fine-grained contactless applications across those categories. These systems provide detailed environmental and physiological observability under conditions where ordinary cameras may be obstructed, poorly illuminated, or intentionally avoided.

7. Drones as the Mobility Layer

Drones transform fixed sensing and directed-energy architecture into a mobile three-dimensional system.

A fixed installation is constrained by its original location. A drone can:

Move closer
Change altitude
Establish line of sight
Reposition around obstacles
Search for a stronger signal
Follow a moving person or vehicle
Orbit a building
Approach from multiple directions
Carry interchangeable payloads
Relay information to another system
Operate as one element of a swarm
Depart after a short operation

Drones are therefore not merely flying cameras. They are movable sensor, communications, computation, and electronic-warfare nodes.

7.1 Airborne detection of breathing and heart activity

Research on UAV-borne radar explicitly describes drones carrying radar systems capable of measuring human respiration and heart activity.

The reported applications include sensing:

Through clothing Through certain building materials Around visual obstructions Under debris In low-visibility environments

The research also discusses drone swarms and tethered UAVs as methods for expanding endurance and multitasking capability.

This creates a direct technical bridge between drones and physiological observation. A drone can contribute more than an image of a person. It can collect minute periodic movement associated with life signs.

7.2 Drones as RF-location systems

Radio-frequency localization uses features such as:

Signal strength
Time of arrival
Time difference of arrival
Angle of arrival
Phase
Channel fingerprints
Multipath structure

Aerial robots can move through space to improve those measurements.

A review of RF localization for aerial and ground robots describes range-based and fingerprint-based methods and connects them to emerging 5G positioning infrastructure.

7.3 Electronic-warfare drones

Electronic-warfare payloads are already being placed on drones.

The British StormShroud system combines the Tekever AR3 UAV with Leonardo's BriteStorm electronic-warfare payload. Its mission is to fly ahead of crewed aircraft and disrupt or deceive hostile radar systems.

This establishes an operational concept in which:

The aircraft is unmanned.
The payload manipulates the electromagnetic environment.
The platform positions itself ahead of another force.
Electronic attack is separated from the human operator.
The drone can be treated as expendable or relocatable.

7.4 Drone swarm phased arrays

One drone has limited antenna size, payload capacity, and available power. Multiple drones can act cooperatively.

A 2025 project experimentally validated UAV-based phased-array antenna structures using dockable RF connectors. The system demonstrated beam steering in stationary and flight-related tests and was designed for airborne communications, radar, and remote sensing.

A 2026 swarm-array paper describes autonomous relay elements cooperatively forwarding a desired signal while canceling interferers through distributed zero-forcing beamforming.

MANY SMALL AIRCRAFT → ONE DISTRIBUTED APERTURE

The system's functional boundary is no longer the individual drone. The swarm becomes the instrument.

7.5 Tethered drones

Tethered UAVs offer a separate deployment model. A tether may provide:

Continuous electrical power
High-capacity data connection
Extended endurance
Fixed-area persistence
Reduced battery dependence
Stable relay positioning

7.6 Drones as targeting assistants

A drone does not need to carry the primary emitter. It can perform only the targeting and measurement functions.

Drone optical/radar tracking → Range and orientation estimate → Coordinates sent to ground emitter → Drone measures target response

Drone RF mapping → Target device association → Fixed infrastructure transmission → Airborne response monitoring

Swarm localization → Distributed phase alignment → Cooperative beam formation → Adaptive repositioning

7.7 Drones as intermittent systems

Drones also change the temporal footprint of an operation. A mobile platform can:

Appear briefly
Collect data

Trajectory prediction

The convergence process can be written as:

D1 + D2 + D3 + ... + Dn → Mperson

where each Di is a partial data source and Mperson is a continuously updated model of the individual.

The model can then guide actuation:

At+1 = π(Mperson, Rt, Et)

where At+1 is the next selected action, Rt is the measured response, Et is the environmental state, and π is the adaptive decision policy.

This creates a closed loop.

The system learns not only what the person does. It learns what changes the person.
