Abstract

Physiological effects have been observed in a human subject in response to stimulation of the skin with weak electromagnetic fields that are pulsed with certain frequencies near ½ Hz or 2.4 Hz, such as to excite a sensory resonance. Many computer monitors and TV tubes, when displaying pulsed images, emit pulsed electromagnetic fields of sufficient amplitudes to cause such excitation. It is therefore possible to manipulate the nervous system of a subject by pulsing images displayed on a nearby computer monitor or TV set. For the latter, the image pulsing may be imbedded in the program material, or it may be overlaid by modulating a video stream, either as an RF signal or as a video signal. The image displayed on a computer monitor may be pulsed effectively by a simple computer program. For certain monitors, pulsed electromagnetic fields capable of exciting sensory resonances in nearby subjects may be generated even as the displayed images are pulsed with subliminal intensity.

Summary of Technical Mechanism

Computer monitors and TV monitors can be made to emit weak low-frequency electromagnetic fields merely by pulsing the intensity of displayed images. Experiments have shown that the ½ Hz sensory resonance can be excited in this manner in a subject near the monitor. The 2.4 Hz sensory resonance can also be excited in this fashion. Hence, a TV monitor or computer monitor can be used to manipulate the nervous system of nearby people.

The various implementations of the invention are adapted to the different sources of video stream, such as video tape, DVD, a computer program, or a TV broadcast through free space or cable. In all implementations, the subject is exposed to the pulsed electromagnetic field that is generated by the monitor as the result of image intensity pulsing. Certain cutaneous nerves of the subject exhibit spontaneous spiking in patterns which, although rather random, contain sensory information at least in the form of average frequency.

Implementation Methods

Hardware Modulation (Video Overlay)

For video feeds (like a DVD or VHS player), the patent describes a "Video Modulator" circuit. This device intercepts the video signal and injects a low-frequency pulse into the luminance (brightness) channel. The circuit slowly shifts the "pseudo-DC" level of the video signal, making the entire image throb slightly brighter and darker at the target frequency (e.g., 0.5 times per second). The change in brightness can be as low as 1–2%, making it subliminal — effectively invisible to the conscious eye but theoretically strong enough to create the electromagnetic pulse.

Software Manipulation

The patent explicitly details how a computer program can achieve the same effect without extra hardware. By manipulating the RGB (Red, Green, Blue) values of the pixels on the screen, software can cause the monitor to emit the required pulsed fields. The software uses a precise timing loop (utilizing the GetTickCount function in Windows) to modulate the pixel intensity in a sine-wave pattern. As illustrated in Figure 9 of the patent, this software does not need to be installed on the target computer; it can be run from a remote server, delivering the pulsed signals via a network or internet connection.

CRT Physics of Emission

In Cathode Ray Tube monitors, the image is drawn by an electron beam (a stream of electrons — current). Pulsing the image intensity pulses this current. Even though the monitor is grounded, this fluctuating current creates a "pulsed electric field" that radiates outward through the glass screen. For LCD monitors, the pulsing is generated by the changing voltage potentials on the electrode plates that control the liquid crystals.

Subliminal Delivery

The patent explicitly acknowledges: "Certain monitors can emit electromagnetic field pulses that excite a sensory resonance in a nearby subject, through image pulses that are so weak as to be subliminal. This is unfortunate since it opens a way for mischievous application of the invention, whereby people are exposed unknowingly to manipulation of their nervous systems for someone else's purposes. Such application would be unethical and is of course not advocated. It is mentioned here in order to alert the public to the possibility of covert abuse that may occur while being online, or while watching TV, a video, or a DVD."
